Political Strategy System

Shape what your empire believes—and what it is capable of doing.

Every empire develops through the interaction of its regime, ideological stances, political agendas, operational directives, and relationships with other powers. Players do not select a fixed faction identity. They create one through repeated decisions.

Core Terms

Regime The structure through which political power is organized and exercised.
Ideological Axes The major philosophical dimensions along which every empire is measured.
Stances An empire’s current positions on the ideological axes, each measured from −10 to +10.
Agendas High-level political efforts that gradually shape the empire’s identity and move its stances.
Directives Concrete actions that produce immediate effects without directly changing ideological stances.

Regimes

A regime describes how an empire governs, not what it believes. It influences the balance between Agenda Points and Directive Points, action efficiency, and other global rules. Regimes may change during play, but such a transition is expected to be rare and significant.

Democracy

Political authority is distributed through representation, participation, and public legitimacy.

Confederation

Power is shared among semi-autonomous members that cooperate while preserving local authority.

Monarchy

Political continuity is concentrated in a sovereign, dynasty, or hereditary institution.

Communism

Political and economic coordination is organized around collective ownership and central planning.

Anarchy

Central authority is weak or absent, with action emerging from local groups, networks, or voluntary organization.

Regimes should change the empire’s overall operating model rather than granting narrow bonuses to a single resource, unit, or action category.

Ideological Axes

Each axis runs from −10 to +10. The labels at each end represent ideological extremes; values near 0 represent moderation, compromise, or unresolved direction.

LibertarianAuthoritarian

Political Freedom: the balance between individual liberty and centralized authority.

IsolationistCooperative

Diplomacy: the empire’s willingness to engage, coordinate, and depend on foreign powers.

SpiritualScientific

Science: whether society privileges faith, tradition, intuition, and meaning, or evidence, research, and technological progress.

IndustrialEcologist

Environment: the balance between extracting material value and preserving ecological systems.

PacifistMilitarist

Military: the importance placed on armed strength and the willingness to use force.

Stances and Ideological Access

A stance is an empire’s current value on an ideological axis. Stances do more than describe identity: they determine which Agendas and Directives the player may access.

Ideological Window

Each stance grants access to actions whose ideological requirements fall within a range around the current value. Under the default rule, most stances provide a five-value access window.

Current Stance Accessible Values Strategic Character
0 −2 to +2 Broad access to moderate and balanced actions.
+4 +2 to +6 Committed, while retaining some moderate options.
−6 −8 to −4 Strong specialization with reduced flexibility.
+10 +7 to +10 Maximum ideological power, but only a four-value window.
−10 −10 to −7 Maximum commitment at the opposite extreme.
Moderation provides breadth. Extremism provides stronger and more distinctive actions, but removes access to moderate, balanced, and opposing actions.

Agendas and Directives

Agendas

Agendas are sustained political efforts. They represent what the government is actively promoting, reinforcing, or normalizing. Spending Agenda Points gradually moves one or more stances.

  • Promote Scientific Education
  • Accelerate Industrialization
  • Expand Civil Liberties
  • Encourage International Cooperation
  • Increase Military Prestige

Directives

Directives are immediate executive actions. They consume Directive Points and produce concrete outcomes, but they do not directly change ideological stances.

  • Construct Infrastructure
  • Recruit Military Forces
  • Launch a Research Expedition
  • Establish a Trade Route
  • Negotiate a Treaty

Action Requirements

Actions may depend on one, two, or three ideological stances. This creates emergent political identities without forcing players into predefined factions.

Single-Stance Actions

The majority of actions depend on one axis and define the core identity of that ideology.

Industrial Expansion Religious Revival Military Recruitment

Dual-Stance Actions

A moderate number of actions emerge from the interaction of two ideological commitments.

Cooperative + Ecologist Scientific + Cooperative Militarist + Authoritarian

Triple-Stance Actions

A small number of rare or defining actions require three compatible stances.

Scientific + Cooperative + Ecologist Authoritarian + Militarist + Industrial

No action requires four or more stances. This keeps the system legible while still enabling highly distinctive empire builds.

Inter-Empire Relations

Empires may interact through trade, contracts, and alliances. Players cannot manually blacklist specific empires from trade or contract participation. Instead, ideological compatibility determines whether interaction is possible and how effective it will be.

Trade

Empires may exchange resources and technology. Compatible stances improve access and outcomes. Severe ideological divergence may block interaction entirely—for example, a fully Libertarian empire may be unable to trade with a fully Authoritarian empire.

Contracts

An empire may post a contract by committing the required resources. Another player spends their own Directive Points to execute it, and both parties share the resulting benefits.

Strategic Consequences

  • Moderate stances make it easier to cooperate with a broad range of empires.
  • Extreme stances unlock stronger actions but may isolate the empire diplomatically.
  • Players may steer toward compatible partners to gain more from cooperation.
  • Players may deliberately diverge from rivals to prevent them from benefiting from interaction.
  • Successful cooperation is always mutually beneficial, even when the gains are asymmetric.

Alliance Gauge

Cooperative actions increase an Alliance Gauge shared by the participating empires. The amount gained depends on the action and on ideological compatibility.

Building Alliance

Trade, completed contracts, joint projects, and other cooperative actions increase the gauge. Similar empires build trust more quickly; opposed empires progress more slowly.

Becoming Allies

Once the gauge crosses a defined threshold, the empires become Allies and unlock additional mechanics. Those alliance-specific mechanics remain to be designed.
